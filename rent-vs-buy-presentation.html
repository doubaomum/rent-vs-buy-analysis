FINDING 01
\n

The Main Driver Changes With Time

\n

Short horizons are timing-led; longer horizons increasingly reflect location and investment compounding.

\n
\n
\n
5 YEARSPurchase Timing
→
15 YEARSPortfolio + Timing + City
→
20 YEARSPortfolio Choice
\n
\n
\n
↗
\n

At the 5 Y holding period, purchase timing is the biggest driver; portfolio choice has an impact similar to a two-percentage-point change in the mortgage rate.
At 15 Y, portfolio choice, purchase timing and city all become significant drivers, well ahead of the mortgage rate.
At 20 Y, portfolio choice is the dominant driver as returns compound (too few purchase cohorts remain to test timing).

\n
The longer the horizon, the more investment compounding matters.

Limitations

How the results should—and should not—be interpreted.

Historical, Not Predictive

\n

The model reconstructs outcomes using observed historical paths for:

Past relationships may change under new economic, policy, demographic, or market conditions.

This is a historical analysis—not a forecast.

Uneven Historical Coverage

\n

Financial Model Only

\n
\n

The model compares financial outcomes, but does not quantify personal and lifestyle value:

\n

↔Renting flexibility

The financially better option is not automatically the better lifestyle choice.

Use the model to understand historical trade-offs—not to predict the future or define the right lifestyle.
